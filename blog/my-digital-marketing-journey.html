My Digital Marketing Learning Journey: What I've Learned So Far

By Amit Rawat · 21 August 2026 · 5 min read

I'm right at the start of this. I spent my career in software QA — testing, finding bugs, thinking about how users actually interact with products. At some point I decided I wanted to shift into digital marketing. This post is an honest snapshot of where I am right now, August 2026. It'll get more interesting as I learn more.

Why I Started

I was a QA professional and I wanted a career change. Digital marketing felt like a natural direction — a lot of the skills transfer more than people expect. QA is fundamentally about understanding user behaviour, spotting what's broken, and making decisions from evidence. That's not a million miles from what good digital marketing requires.

But I also knew I couldn't just say "my QA skills transfer" without actually understanding the field. So I decided to learn properly — not just take a course and move on, but build something real and work through it hands-on.

What I've Done So Far

I'm at the very beginning, so the list is short. But these are real things, not just things I've read about:

  • Built this website from scratch — HTML, CSS, JavaScript, no frameworks — specifically as a practice environment for SEO and analytics
  • Deployed it properly on Netlify with a real URL, sitemap, robots.txt, and structured data
  • Written five blog articles covering GTM, SEO basics, GA4 vs GTM, and conversion tracking — learning by writing
  • Set up the site's meta tags, Open Graph data, and canonical URLs correctly
  • Got to grips with the theory behind GTM and GA4, even before installing either

Reading about digital marketing is like reading about swimming. At some point you have to get in the water.

What's Actually Clicked So Far

The GA4 vs GTM distinction was genuinely confusing to me for a while. Once it clicked — GA4 is where data lives, GTM is how it gets there — a lot of other things made more sense. It's a simple idea but most explanations bury it under too much jargon.

I also found that my QA instinct helps here. When I read about conversion tracking, I naturally think "but how do you verify this is actually firing correctly?" That kind of scepticism turns out to be exactly the right mindset for analytics work.

What I'm Still Figuring Out

Most of it, honestly. Keyword research feels overwhelming — there's a lot of conflicting advice and without real data from a live site it's hard to practise. Paid search (Google Ads) is still a complete unknown. And I haven't actually installed GTM or GA4 on this site yet — that's the next real step.

What's Next

The immediate priorities are getting GTM installed on this site, setting up GA4 through it, and submitting to Google Search Console. Once I have real data coming in, the learning will accelerate — there's only so much you can understand in theory.

I'll update this post as things develop. Right now it's a short post because it's an early stage. That feels more honest than padding it out.

💡 If you're just starting out:

The best thing I can say is: start with something real. Don't just take courses and read articles. Build a website, set up analytics, write some content, and see what happens. Learning by doing is orders of magnitude more effective than learning by watching. You can read SEO for Beginners and What Is Google Tag Manager? as starting points — then do something with what you learn.

I'll keep updating this site as I learn. If you're on a similar journey, I'd genuinely love to hear from you — get in touch.
